# A86 plan — collar and compact torso interface

## Hypothesis

The frozen A85 S12 head can become a viable whole-plush receiver if a separately
constructed soft collar and compact torso cover its hidden underside with broad
fabric contact. Testing that interface directly is more result-oriented than
continuing bare-head underside edits that repeatedly damaged passing
silhouettes.

## Immutable parent and scope

- Copy exact `A85_head_cage_s12.blend`, SHA-256
  `982da6404ea6edcbb4432903e67dad4ee5c130a203a5a5727a374b773fc9ad8a`.
- Freeze `A85_HeadCushionCage`: no vertex, modifier, transform, or material
  change in this attempt.
- Keep the attached canonical front, canonical turn, and physical references,
  six fixed cameras, 25 cm whole-plush scale contract, and separated asset and
  review collections.
- Add only a white collar and red compact torso receiver. Do not add sleeves,
  skirt, legs, feet, hair, bow, face, materials beyond diagnostic clay colors,
  or rig controls.

## Direct authoring method

Construct each new form as a sparse named quad cage in Blender. Use explicit
front/rear panels and a shallow side edge rather than spheres, capsules,
annuli, or a generated axial loft. Subdivision may soften the cages. One
coordinator owns artistic geometry; scripts may only prepare the copy,
cameras, metadata, and fixed renders.

The collar should read as a low soft fabric band under the broad face, not a
hard ring or neck. The torso should be compact and subordinate to the head,
with a softly stuffed front, shallow side depth, and enough upper width to
support the collar without becoming a human shoulder/chest.

## Interface gates

Keep the coupon only if all required views show:

1. complete coverage of the S12 underside cavity;
2. broad continuous collar/head and collar/torso contact;
3. no visible gap, interpenetrating boundary, dark cavity, or floating band;
4. no movement of the frozen S12 silhouette beyond one pixel in registered
   component-ID comparison;
5. compact Fumo proportions rather than a human neck/torso; and
6. separate reusable objects with manifold intended surfaces and intentional
   transforms.

Render a front/profile pair first with repository-pinned Blender. If it passes,
render rear and both three-quarters from the same immutable snapshot. Return
early and change only the failing interface if the collar reads as a ring,
the torso as a capsule/block, or the receiver hides the head by overlap rather
than plausible contact.
