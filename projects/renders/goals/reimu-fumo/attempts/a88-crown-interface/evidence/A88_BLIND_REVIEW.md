# A88 crown-interface implementation-blind review

## Review boundary

- Stage: neutral, untextured partial plush sculpt.
- Candidate evidence: the fixed-camera `front`, `three_quarter`, and
  `three_quarter_mirror` renders from A88 S02, S03, and S04.
- Deliberately withheld: Blender files, topology, object names, author intent,
  numeric diagnostics, and the edit history between candidates.
- Explicit scope supplied to the reviewer: two crown panels behind an existing
  front fringe and cheek locks; lower rear hair and the bow are absent.
- Primary references: `canonical_front_25cm.png` for frontal construction and
  `canonical_turn_180.gif` for crown depth, side/rear layer order, and panel
  silhouette.
- Supporting references: `physical_front.png` and `physical_side.png` for
  stuffing and fabric-edge behavior; `clean_front.png` for the neutral front
  silhouette; `turn.gif` and `sofa.gif` as qualitative continuity and softness
  checks.
- Limitation: the candidate packet contains no direct side or rear render.
  Therefore the rear interface cannot pass a multi-view checkpoint from this
  evidence even if an oblique looks plausible.

The review judges only visible pixels. Missing lower rear hair and bow are not
counted as defects by themselves. Their receiving edge must nevertheless read
as a deliberate overlap interface rather than an accidental open shell.

## Absolute verdict

**Reject S02, S03, and S04 as exact provisional survivors.** None yet reads as
the reference's shallow, sewn, softly stuffed hair shell with a clean receiving
interface for the later rear panels. S03 is the closest *repair basis*, but its
visible crown opening means the exact S03 state is not safe to preserve as a
checkpoint.

This is not a full-model approval judgment. With the face, bow, body, and rear
hair absent, same-subject recognition and full identity fidelity are not
assessable. The crown module itself is judged absolutely against the photographed
construction.

## Five largest discrepancies

Ordered by impact across the strongest basis, S03, with the other candidates
noted where they are worse:

1. **Open center-top seam.** S03 shows a conspicuous white star-shaped hole at
   the crown seam. S04 retains a smaller opening. The photographed crown has a
   closed sewn center seam, not visible scalp/face fabric through the top.
2. **The lower rear receiving edge is a rigid horizontal cut.** In both
   obliques it ends as a long, nearly ruler-straight free edge. The references
   show overlapping stuffed hair leaves and seam-controlled curvature. Even
   before lower rear hair is added, its receiver should turn under or present a
   controlled curved overlap boundary.
3. **Crown volume is not yet the photographed shallow cushion.** S02 is too
   flat and angular; S03/S04 swing toward a smooth monolithic dome. The physical
   references show slightly flattened planes, low-frequency stuffing
   asymmetry, and seam tension—not a tabletop slab or a uniform cap.
4. **The crown-to-fringe junction reads as stacked hard layers.** A broad,
   almost continuous ridge crosses the forehead. The reference does have a
   front layer over a crown mass, but the transition is seated and fabric-soft,
   with local compression instead of a uniform armor-like ledge.
5. **Temple continuity is fragile or broken.** S02 and S03 retain more frontal
   coverage but terminate in abrupt vertical steps; S04 visibly exposes white
   gaps at both temples and separates the crown from the cheek locks. The
   reference maintains brown hair coverage through this transition.

## Helmet/cap and interface gate

- **S02 helmet/card risk:** categorical failure. Its planar top and squared
  wings overwhelm any fabric cue.
- **S03 cap risk:** material but locally repairable. The softened crown mass is
  plausible, yet the open top and uniform forehead ridge still expose it as a
  separate shell.
- **S04 cap risk:** categorical in contact/occlusion. The retracted sides and
  high rear opening make the shell visibly float.
- **Front coverage:** S03 is strongest; S04 regresses it with bilateral white
  temple gaps.
- **Center seam:** no exact state passes. S02 is closed but faceted; S03/S04
  are visibly open.
- **Rear interface:** no exact state passes, and no direct rear camera is
  available. The obliques already show a dead-straight exposed boundary rather
  than a controlled future overlap.

## Smallest defensible next cycle

Freeze S03 only as an **unaccepted comparison basis**. Do not promote it as a
survivor. First make one local, coupled seam correction that closes the
center-top opening without flattening the soft crown volume or changing the
existing fringe/temple coverage. Keep it only if:

1. the white opening disappears in front and both obliques;
2. the center seam remains visible as a soft closed seam rather than a welded
   ridge;
3. S03's temple coverage and crown height do not regress; and
4. a new direct side/rear check confirms that the lower edge can receive the
   later rear panel without floating or exposing the head.

Only after that closed-seam candidate survives should a separate bounded cycle
reshape the long rear free edge into a deliberate overlap interface. Combining
both changes now would prevent attribution and repeat the S03-to-S04 trade-off.
