# A87 S02 implementation-blind hair-frame review

## Review conditions

I reviewed only the S00, S01, and S02 front and mirrored three-quarter renders
against every tracked reference before consulting object names or topology.
The canonical front controls frontal identity and contour; the canonical
180-degree turn controls depth, layer order, and hidden-side construction.
The physical front/side, clean front, sofa turn, and supplied four-frame turn
were used as supporting fabric and continuity evidence.

This is an absolute review of the front-hair module at the neutral-sculpt
stage, not a judgment of technical cleanliness or relative effort.

## Absolute verdict

**Reject S02.** It is the strongest of the three candidates, but it still
reads as a smooth helmet front with two inflated tubes rather than sewn plush
hair. The candidate is not suitable as an approval checkpoint or as a base for
detail/material work.

A major intended-medium and construction failure is present, so the absolute
gate fails regardless of relative improvement.

## Why S02 reads as helmet plus tubes

1. **One continuous dome owns both crown and fringe.** From both
   three-quarter views the frontal hair has a round, uninterrupted surface
   from the crown to the brow, then stops on a nearly vertical plane. The
   references instead show a shallow front fabric face whose cut lower edge
   overlaps distinct side/rear sheets; it does not read as a padded shell
   wrapped over a head.
2. **The side locks are inflated in every direction.** Their long parallel
   sides, broad semicircular tips, and soft cylindrical shading make them look
   like tubes or plush limbs. The references show thin, broad felt/fleece
   panels: a readable front face, a narrow edge, an angular/tapered lower end,
   and a root tucked behind the front fringe.
3. **The lower fringe is a smoothed three-scallop opening.** S02's central
   point is broad and rounded, with two large clean arches exposing the face.
   The controlling front has a narrower, longer central wedge and shallower,
   irregular adjacent flaps. The turntable confirms these are overlapping cut
   panel edges, not one sinusoidal opening in a continuous cap.

## Candidate ranking

1. **S02 — reject, retain only as diagnostic evidence.** Best front contour
   of the three, but the same dome-and-capsule representation remains visible
   in every view.
2. **S01 — reject.** Softer than S00, but collapses the fringe into one broad
   central scallop and makes both locks especially cylindrical.
3. **S00 — reject.** Its multiple points superficially recall hair, but the
   hard, uniformly extruded W-card and faceted side paddles read as armor.

## Three highest-leverage structural changes

1. **Replace only the front dome with a shallow constructed face panel.** Keep
   the frozen head receiver, but make the front hair a broad, nearly planar
   lightly stuffed sheet with restrained convexity. Its upper/root boundary
   should terminate into a future crown/bow seam rather than wrapping over and
   owning the whole dome. Discriminating test: in three-quarter view, the
   panel must show a broad face and a thin edge instead of a hemispherical
   highlight and vertical helmet cutoff.
2. **Rebuild each side lock as a tapered fabric leaf.** Use a broad root
   overlapped behind the front panel, a mostly planar face, narrow finite edge,
   slight outward drape/twist, and a blunt angular lower tip. Avoid axial or
   uniformly rounded cross-sections. Discriminating test: front and
   three-quarter renders must show face-versus-edge transitions and a clear
   root depth order, never a capsule silhouette.
3. **Redraw the lower fringe as a few asymmetric overlapping flaps.** Make the
   center wedge narrower and longer, keep adjacent brow flaps shallower, and
   vary their overlap/root tension rather than smoothing them into three
   equal arches. Discriminating test: the canonical-front face opening should
   match the characteristic central wedge while the three-quarter view still
   shows separate thin free edges without floating or intersection.

These are macro construction changes. Seams, fibers, wrinkles, materials, and
additional hair parts cannot rescue S02 before they pass.
