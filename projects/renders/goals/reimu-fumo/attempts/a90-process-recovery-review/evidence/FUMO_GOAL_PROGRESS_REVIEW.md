# Reimu Fumo goal progress review through A89

Observed: 2026-09-01

## Executive verdict

The goal has made substantial progress in reference capture, reproducible
rendering, source preservation, rejection discipline, and local geometric
experiments. It has **not** made comparable progress toward the requested
visible result. No full-goal criterion passes, no complete sculpt is accepted,
and the tracked reusable Blend remains the same rejected migrated model.

Recent work improved one experimental head scaffold and two local hair
interfaces. It did not produce a complete plush that is visibly better than
the older protected rung 003 comparator. The user's perception of little
recent progress is therefore correct.

The decisive process failure is not Blender startup, renderer throughput,
available memory, or too few agents. Those bottlenecks were reduced. The
current failure is **visual decomposition and sequencing**: locally incomplete
modules were optimized and provisionally frozen without immediately proving
that they improved a complete recognizable Fumo. Mechanical gates such as
coverage, zero-gap pixels, topology, and protected-region stability became
easier to pass than the actual likeness and plush-construction gates.

Decision-review verdict: **revise**. Do not resume A89 tuning, do not treat
A85/A87/A88 as a production foundation without one whole-context test, and do
not weaken acceptance. Run a short whole-plush bakeoff described in
`FUMO_RECOVERY_PLAN.md`.

## What genuinely improved

### 1. Reference and comparison quality

The canonical 25 cm front still, the true 180-degree turn, physical front and
side images, normalized landmark contract, fixed cameras, reference ownership,
and uncertainty rules are useful durable assets. The reference shortage is no
longer the dominant problem.

### 2. Safety and reproducibility

The tracked model and Sisyphus scene remained protected while experiments ran
on exact isolated copies. Candidate hashes, fixed render specifications,
component-ID passes, clean-reopen checks, and explicit keep/undo decisions
greatly reduce accidental regression and allow exact resumption.

### 3. Feedback latency

The authoring/render path became fast enough:

- A79 took 94 minutes 30 seconds to first whole-character pixels;
- A80 reduced that to 50 minutes 41.672 seconds but still missed its 12-minute
  target;
- A82 produced its controlling pair in about 3 minutes 35 seconds;
- A87 reports live edits in seconds and a pinned three-view packet in about
  18 seconds; and
- several isolated render packets complete in roughly 11-33 seconds.

The remaining delay is mostly before authoring, before first pixels, or before
the decisive review—not GPU or Blender render time.

### 4. Narrow local geometry knowledge

- A85 S12 is a more plausible shallow receiver than the prior eggs, spheres,
  and rounded cubes. It passes its bounded bare-head silhouette gates.
- A87 S04 establishes useful ownership for a short fringe and two cheek locks.
- A88 S07 closes the upper crown gap without moving A87's protected front
  pixels and retains a bounded rear handoff rim.

These are legitimate experiments and reusable evidence. They remain
provisional because their combined whole-head plush read is weak and their
complete-character value has never been demonstrated.

### Whole-character scope

Since the A83 reset, work moved from a failed complete model to a head-only
scene, then to progressively smaller interfaces inside that head. A84-A89
contain no current face, bow, seated torso, skirt, sleeves, feet, or complete
rear hair. This removes the very relationships—head-to-body ratio, bow span,
seated compression, skirt/foot occlusion, side depth, and rear mass—that make
the subject recognizable.

### Animation and delivery

No rig or topology work on a rejected visual parent can establish animation
readiness. The tracked reusable Blend remains byte-identical at SHA-256
`489213b7d0a62feb5c6b60ce36483757638886af3a4af25efa41e402e46b1d76`.
The recent head modules live only as ignored experimental snapshots; none has
entered an approved standalone asset.

That is also a retention weakness. The exact A85, A87, and A88 Blend snapshots
are hash-bound and currently recoverable from ignored `out/` storage, but they
are neither remotely durable nor deterministically regenerable from tracked
source. Any branch selected as a real recovery parent must receive an explicit
non-temporary Git LFS checkpoint before its ignored scratch tree is disposable.

## Why recent progress stalled

### 1. The work unit stopped matching the user-visible result

The process correctly learned to make smaller reversible modules, but applied
that rule too literally. The minimum useful context became too small. Passing
a receiver silhouette, fringe free edge, gap count, or crown overlap did not
show a better Fumo. A small module is valuable only when its first packet also
contains enough whole-result context to prove that it moves the goal.

### 2. Local gates substituted for absolute quality

A85/A87/A88 were retained for narrow reasons while their subject-level plush
scores remained roughly 2.5-3.5/10. “Zero exposed pixels,” “unchanged protected
ROI,” “mechanically composable,” and “correct owner” are interface facts, not
evidence that the forms resemble the reference. Later work then routed around
weak pixels because they had become frozen.

### 3. Composition was assumed rather than tested

A85, A87, and A88 are nested snapshots, but no packet adds even cheap face,
bow, body, and rear-mass proxies around A88 and compares the complete result
with rung 003. The chain's compatibility is mechanical; its visual
composability is unproven.

### 4. The first independent veto arrived too late

A89 produced S00-S06 before the decisive blind finding was integrated. The
states changed gaps and overlap but converged on the same cape/fin category.
This repeated earlier attempts in which several patches followed a first
packet that had already falsified the representation.

### 5. Process artifacts displaced pixels

The record contains valuable evidence, but planning, contracts, metrics,
review machinery, and archival reports repeatedly grew before a crude visual
probe. A79 is the clearest example: 94.5 minutes to pixels followed by 29.115
seconds of rendering and an immediate rejection. More documentation could not
repair the hypothesis that those first pixels disproved.

### 6. Authoring modality and artistic control remain mismatched

Analytic generation is excellent for camera calibration, landmarks,
repeatable topology checks, IDs, and renders. It repeatedly produced boxes,
eggs, masks, cards, and capes when asked to generate the organic, asymmetric
plush itself. Conversely, the claimed direct-sculpt route has not yet proven
broad controllable input: A68/A71 produced local dents and A76 lacked reliable
pointer control. A high-resolution sculpt is not justified until one cheap
input-support coupon succeeds.

### 7. Parallelism was applied around—not through—the bottleneck

Independent reviewers, measurers, and process auditors improved evidence, but
the visual hypothesis remained one serial authoring lane. More agent slots or
parallel reports cannot compensate for a weak base representation. The next
parallelism must compare two genuinely distinct complete visual hypotheses on
isolated copies, with one coordinator choosing the survivor.

## Decision review

### Actual outcome and constraints

Produce a great, reference-faithful, 25 cm Reimu Fumo that can be reused and
animated. Preserve the supplied reference authority, standalone approval
stages, tracked source, and no-external-model-generation constraint.

### Strongest case for continuing the module chain

It is the only recent lineage with exact provisional survivors and clear
object ownership. Preserving it avoids discarding hard-won measurements and
could yield deformation-friendly panels.

### Strongest reason to reject that as the sole strategy

The current assembled pixels still read as a generic cap on a rounded block,
and the first missing owner immediately became a cape. Continuing locally can
consume many apparent module wins before discovering that the whole subject is
still worse than rung 003.

### Credible alternatives

1. Continue modular assembly unchanged: low immediate whole-image leverage;
   rejected except for one bounded control test.
2. Fresh complete low-detail, construction-aware blockout: highest immediate
   whole-image leverage; selected as primary.
3. Direct manual sculpt then retopology: high potential but authoring control
   unproven; conditional on a 10-15 minute input coupon.
4. Stop or lower acceptance: not justified while the most direct whole-plush
   bakeoff has not been run; rejected absent new user direction.

### Verdict

**Revise.** Freeze A85/A87/A88 as an immutable control branch. Build one fresh
complete construction-aware blockout as the primary branch. Compare both in
the same fixed whole-subject packet. Only the winner may receive one macro
refinement. Do not resume A89, do not start detail/retopo/material/rig work,
and do not weaken the goal.

## Evidence that would change this verdict

- A one-packet A88 context test that clearly beats the fresh complete blockout
  would justify modular continuation, but only with whole-context gates after
  every kept module.
- A broad-input sculpt coupon that produces predictable low-frequency
  deformations would justify direct sculpt as the preferred refinement method.
- Three genuinely distinct complete macro representations failing the same
  calibrated subject-level gate would justify pausing for an explicit scope or
  feasibility decision; it would not silently lower acceptance.
- Faster rendering, more reports, or more agent slots alone would not change
  the verdict.
