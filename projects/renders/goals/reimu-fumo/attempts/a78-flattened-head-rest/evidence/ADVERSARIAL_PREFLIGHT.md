# A78 adversarial preflight

## Verdict: NO-GO

Return before Blender mutation and before freezing this text as a goal attempt.
The proposed head-rest-only boundary is the previously recorded
`D-HIDDEN-OPT` / `D-FACE-MASK` boundary with a different topology description.
It does not yet establish a materially new way to form the visible surface,
and it leaves the rejected hair shell in control of nearly all head pixels.

The minimum useful correction is to reverse the boundary: preserve
`Head_Cushion_Manual_Target` and the seated face witnesses, then replace the
complete helmet-owning crown/rear hair field with explicitly authored paired
fabric surfaces. A smaller root-band coupon may preflight that interface
first. A purpose-built head rest should be deferred until a visible-hair
candidate proves that the existing receiver is the concrete blocker.

No Blender file or goal record was changed in this review.

## Outcome and strongest case for A78

The real outcome is not a mechanically clean hidden cushion. It is a whole
Reimu plush whose head reads as a shallow stuffed support plus separately
draping hair in front, both three-quarters, both profiles, and rear. The next
work unit must be able to improve those pixels and to rule out the recurring
helmet, mattress, egg, mask, card, cavity, and detached-root failures.

The strongest case for A78 is legitimate: A77 could not fit its new rest
surface to the frozen face witnesses, while a front panel, rear panel, and
perimeter gusset sound closer to sewn construction than a sphere or a
Subdivision/Solidify mantle. A denser front control field could also keep the
eyes and mouth on a calmer plane than A75 C0.

That case does not survive the prior evidence. It explains how A78 might make
a better local beige object, but not why that object is the next observable
pixel owner or why its final surface will differ from the retired analytic
cushion family.

## Is front/rear/gusset materially new?

As drafted, no.

A73 already supplied independently controlled front reach, rear reach, width,
crown and underside closures, a quiet face-side span, a high rear maximum, a
lower-rear tuck, and pole-free cap patches. It was not literally divided into
three named sewing pieces, but the evaluated object was still the same class
of artifact A78 currently specifies: a deterministic, manually parameterized,
closed smooth volume. More decisively, A56B *was* literally divided into
independent front and rear patterns plus a tapered crown/side/chin gusset and
relaxed under closed-cloth pressure. Its technically stable quad-panel result
was still a uniformly taut rounded foam block/mattress. A78 supplies neither a
new static form mechanism relative to A73 nor a new material mechanism
relative to A56B.

More front sampling is also not decisive novelty. A73 had a dense enough
control structure to pass all of its numeric gates, yet the absolute pixel
review rejected its uniform inflated read. The missing evidence is not vertex
density; it is a demonstrated, source-controlled distinction between the calm
face field, late cheek roll, crown behavior, rear support, and the separately
visible brown layers.

### Cloth pressure/relaxation is also NO-GO for this boundary

Cloth does not reopen A78:

- A56B is the direct prior test. Its two authorized closed-pressure solves
  used actual front/rear cuts and a sewn tapered gusset. Configuration A was a
  button-tufted pillow with a rear drawstring crater; the cleaner, highly
  stable `17 x 17` quad-panel configuration B was a taut rounded foam
  block/mattress. Its terminal report closed the two-configuration branch.
- A14's closed cloth sack did not reach its terminal numeric gate: from frames
  80 to 90 its maximum bounding-box change remained `0.05419` and RMS motion
  `0.06987`, despite nearly stable volume.
- A42 bracketed relaxation on a closed stuffed pocket. The conservative solve
  made no readable artistic change; the released solve shrank width `31.4%`,
  grew depth `161.2%`, and collapsed into a rubber cup.

Pressure alone therefore brackets three known outcomes here: no effect,
nonconvergence/collapse, or uniform foam inflation. Reopening a simulated
receiver would require a materially larger construction redesign—separate
crown/side/chin ownership or darts, measured differential seam lengths, a
distinct compact rear closure, and a lower receiving panel/constraint—as
A56B itself records. That is not a minimum correction to A78 and still would
not fix the hidden-pixel boundary. Do not schedule another pressure or
relaxation probe in this attempt.

## Exact minimum corrections before a new attempt may proceed

### Preferred critical-path correction: hair-only

1. Replace the A78 objective and hypothesis. Test whether explicitly authored
   paired hair surfaces can remove `D-HEAD-HELMET` over the unchanged receiver;
   do not claim that a new receiver is prerequisite.
2. Preserve `Head_Cushion_Manual_Target`, all seven face witnesses, bow, body,
   clothing, cameras, lights, and materials. Hide the six helmet/rear owners
   from A77's boundary, not the receiver:

   - `A44 continuous hair cap with smooth opening`
   - `A42 Left asymmetric rear lock`
   - `A42 Off-center main rear lock`
   - `A42 Short right rear lock`
   - `A42 Main lock left seated seam`
   - `A42 Main lock right seated seam`

   The new candidate must provide crown, profile, and complete rear coverage
   before its first render; no bald-rear stage is reviewable.
3. Author front and back surfaces, transverse camber, soft edge roll, and
   asymmetric tip/root tension explicitly. Do not copy a near-concentric
   receiver swath, use uniform Solidify as the only volume, or derive the
   visible surface from another receiver loft.
4. Fit the evaluated root bands to the existing receiver and retained fringe
   context. Require pointwise signed clearance, zero triangle crossings, at
   least `80%` of each declared root band inside its numeric clearance band,
   and no one-vertex/minimum-distance shortcut. Record every band and limit in
   the plan before building.
5. Render front, both three-quarters, the worse profile, and rear in whole
   context at the first pixel gate. Require an unambiguous preference over
   rung 003 in every affected view, macro silhouette at least `6/10`,
   construction at least `5/10`, complete rear occlusion, and no helmet,
   canopy, card, floating root, cavity, or beige leak. One categorical failure
   is an immediate reset.

### If face/support work is intentionally chosen later

1. Rename it as a **face-carrier coupon**, not a reusable complete head-rest
   validation, unless hair is removed enough for the rear panel and gusset to
   be observable.
2. Either include the seven face appliques in the disposable mutation boundary
   or state why frozen seating does not force the old normal field. A75 already
   identified `front carrier + seven appliques` as the honest smaller boundary
   when frozen witnesses overconstrain the surface.
3. Do not use pressure/relaxation as the novelty claim: A56B already ran the
   explicit sewn front/rear/gusset proof and rejected it. A future simulation
   would need the larger construction-ownership reset listed above and a new
   adversarial review; it is outside A78.
4. Replace vague gates such as “declared band” and “bounded depth” with exact
   per-witness signed-clearance bands, band coverage, crossing checks, and an
   observable pixel claim. Do not use hidden receiver dimensions as likeness
   evidence when the references do not expose them.
5. Close the coupon if rung 003 remains preferred in any controlling exposed
   view or if the result repeats `D-FACE-MASK`; do not promote it on mechanical
   validity or use it as an assumed parent for hair.

## Decision

**NO-GO on the current A78 decision and plan.** The decisive evidence is the
combination of A73's mechanically successful but visually generic closed
receiver, A75's explicit receiver-only boundary failure and terminal defer,
and A77's finding that the visible helmet-owning hair field is the next pixel
owner. A56B independently closes the supposed sewn-pressure novelty. The
verdict changes only if the boundary is rewritten to the hair owner
(preferred), or if new interface evidence first proves that the existing
receiver prevents an otherwise passing hair construction.
