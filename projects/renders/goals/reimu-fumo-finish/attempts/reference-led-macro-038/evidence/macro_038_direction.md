Scope: visual construction review only, 2026-09-05. No model edits.
Read the reference-fidelity skill, construction guide, and absolute gate.
Inspected canonical_front_25cm, physical_front, physical_side, and 037

Canonical front normalization: Wh = 368 px is a reasonable head-envelope
reference on the 1000 px image, approximately x303 through x671. Boundary
placement has several pixels of uncertainty from soft edges and hair locks;

## Five construction changes, ordered by impact

1. Rebuild the complete head/hair mass as a stuffed cushion. The 037 side
   reads as a tall, heavily projecting helmet, with a nearly circular upper
   mass and isolated pale lower face. Make the front a broad gently bulging
   panel, rounded cheek corners, flattened lower seam, and a soft side gusset.
   Attach thin hair to that surface. Preserve real side depth; do not extrude
   a front outline into a wafer. The canonical crown is approximately y232,
   chin y590, giving about 0.97 Wh crown-to-chin. Fringe tips and side locks
   are separate landmarks, not reasons to enlarge the cushion.

2. Replace helmet fringe and dangling hair straps with sewn fabric shapes.
   The reference has one continuous brown forehead coverage, a broad central
   sweep, thin pointed temples, and filled teardrop side locks below ties.
   The 037 forehead looks like a thick shell and its side pieces like narrow
   hanging ribbons. Keep fringe thin and near-flush, with gentle edge roll;
   give lower locks width and rounded fill through their middle before taper.
   Seat them at the head seam. Never make separately inflated forehead lobes
   or use the same constant-thickness strip for every lock.

3. Reconstruct the compact body and skirt from cloth panels. The 037 chest is
   a red rectangular pedestal and skirt a smooth conical lampshade. Canonical
   visible bodice is roughly y592-699, 0.29 Wh tall, with a soft taper and
   collar hiding its top. Skirt extends approximately y691-772, 0.22 Wh,
   with seated compression, broad shallow folds, and an irregular hem.
   Put a small stuffed body inside; drape front/back panels around it with
   curvature changing from waist to lap. Front width alone cannot cure a
   rotationally symmetric cone. Check side for a seated lap, rear contact,
   and feet emerging beneath the front rather than a hollow bell.

4. Build bow leaves and sleeves with folded-cloth volume and attached roots.
   The 037 bow is extremely taut and regular; its tails look like clipped
   sheets. Keep the canonical broad span but gather the loops into a buried
   central root, opening into softly bowed leaves with rounded seam edges.
   The physical side shows cloth bending out of the crown attachment, not
   two plates on a stalk. Sleeves need front/back panels around the short
   stuffed arm, soft side seams, and a cuff with modest thickness. Avoid the
   present trumpet/tube appearance and mechanically repeated trim waves.

5. Restore the facial and lower-body overlaps after the macro forms settle.
   Canonical eyes are roughly centered x416/x558 and y477, spacing 0.39 Wh;
   chin is about y590. Fit flat embroidery to the face rather than leaving
   a thick eye/mask layer. Crown-to-eye placement matters more than adding
   eyelashes. Feet should be small rounded plush ends, substantially hidden
   by the hem, with a short pale fabric transition. The 037 smooth black
   ovals and dark horizontal under-skirt band read as separate plastic parts.

## Skeptical verdict on the proposed rebuild

Conditional support: traced front proportions, a shallow pillow-front head,
close-fitting thin hair, and compact clothing directly address the largest
037 failures. However, tracing guarantees only a projection. It is easy to
replace the helmet with a cardboard face, thin hair with cut paper, and the
cone with a flat skirt fan. Those would still fail plush construction.

Require front and side simultaneously: gently convex face, visible rounded
gusset, hair following the cushion, bow roots buried at the crown, garment
panels supported by the torso/lap, and compressed contact at the seated base.
Use broad seam-driven curvature rather than global sphere smoothing or
random wrinkles. Do not spend this cycle on fibers, tiny stitches, or rigging.

Fast rejection tests: silhouette-only front matches crown/chin/eye, body,
bow, hem, and foot landmarks; neutral-lit three-quarter reads as stuffed
fabric with soft contacts; side has neither a sphere shell nor a wafer face,
neither a skirt cone nor a skirt card. A successful front overlay does not
override a failing side view. No absolute acceptance is issued here.
