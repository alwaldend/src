# Macro 039 image-only review

Verdict: neither front nor side is ready to advance beyond macro construction.
The character is recognizable, but the side view exposes unresolved primitive
construction. These judgments concern volume, silhouette, and attachment;
simple color labels and unfinished materials are not counted as failures.

Reviewed only canonical_front_25cm.png, physical_front.png,
physical_side.png, and macro_039_fast_review/front.png and side.png.

1. Head and hair form a tall, flat-ended drum in profile. The crown becomes
   a nearly horizontal shelf, the back becomes a vertical wall, and a second
   rigid slab is visible behind it. The reference has a rounded stuffed head
   under hair that falls into separate soft ends. Front also reads as a narrow
   capsule between a very wide bow. Rebuild the head profile around a rounded
   crown and curved occiput, soften the lower rear transition, and make the
   back hair continue that envelope before separating at its lower ends.
   Preserve the front facial opening while adding lateral cheek breadth.

2. The seated body reads as a little upright torso standing on a rigid skirt
   dome. In profile the red shoulder mass is bulbous and narrow, the skirt
   projects rearward as a smooth ramp, and the pale leg is an exposed straight
   cylinder. The reference has a compact settled body, draped cloth around the
   seat, and legs nestled into the hem. Lower and integrate the torso into the
   seated mass, break the skirt's even cone into a softer lap and gathered
   sides, and nest the leg roots under cloth. Establish this silhouette before
   adding more hem ripples.

3. Sleeves and bow tails still read as geometric appendages. The front sleeves
   are straight white trapezoids with small balls poking out; in profile the
   cuff becomes a clean ring framing a sphere. The reference sleeves are broad
   cloth bells with compressed openings and hands seated within them. The bow
   tails form two taut, matching diagonal paddles, where the reference tails
   hang and turn behind the hair. Give sleeves volume through their length,
   a soft folded cuff, and a recessed hand; turn the tails in depth and let
   their lower portions droop. Retain the broad upper bow silhouette, which
   already communicates the character.

Priority: repair the head side profile first, then the seated body and sleeve
construction. Require coherent rounded plush volumes in both views before
advancing to surface detail. Front-only refinement would conceal the main
construction problem.
