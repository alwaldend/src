## Minimal credible reset

Separate the roles currently carried by the same lower wrap:

1. **Protected face-side cap/opening.** Preserve complete visible front/fringe/graphic faces and their meaningful boundary contours, not only selected vertex coordinates. Keep its lower side closure behind the existing face framing. Root's source-boundary inspection must identify which mixed faces currently bridge this protected region to the rear ends; ancestry alone is not a visibility test.
2. **Rear cloth with its own free contour.** Remove/rebuild that lower bridging strip in the hair cover. Give the posterior central section and broad laps independently designed tapered lower ends and deliberate overlap order. Their lower contour must not return across the underside to the fixed front opening. The exact number of physical pieces is not recoverable from these views; a central section plus overlapping lateral laps is a modeling choice, not a photographed topology claim.
3. **Closed thickness and hidden support.** Close each newly exposed cloth contour from outer skin to its own inner skin with a narrow rim; a free *cloth midsurface edge* does not mean an open/nonmanifold mesh. Preserve the closed head cushion, fitting only concealed posterior support where the new cloth actually requires room. Do not remove the cushion's structural underside merely because the visible hair needs a free hem. Upper roots remain attached/seated in explicitly retained regions, not floating after a split.

This is a boundary/correspondence reset, not necessarily a change of global mesh genus or a mandate to split every object. In principle a sufficiently redesigned closed wrap could hide its connecting surface and reproduce the same exterior. But that would require moving/rebuilding the transition itself: it is not the tested rear-only deformation with fixed front support. Retaining the present bridging role offers no evidence-based advantage and repeats the coupling that produced both collision and silhouette failures.

A cut alone is insufficient. Keeping102's straight trailing edge and rounded central outline after separating them would preserve the card/apron appearance. The new end contours and broad surface curvature must be designed with the new boundary: supported at the roots, softly changing direction through the cloth face, and tapering into staggered ends rather than an extruded rectangle or a continuous rounded bowl. Do not add noisy folds to disguise that macro shape.
