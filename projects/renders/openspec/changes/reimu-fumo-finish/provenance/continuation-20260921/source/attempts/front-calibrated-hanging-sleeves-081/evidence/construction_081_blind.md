   the shell impression, but changing roughness cannot create the missing edges.
   A substantial useful change would reconstruct the lower side/rear cap as a
   few broad, lightly stuffed fabric lobes joined to the continuous crown.
   As an initial bounded hypothesis, raise selected inner lobe ends by about
   0.06-0.10 Wh and turn their last third inward by 0.02-0.04 Wh, retaining the
   longer outer silhouette. Use shallow overlaps and soft seam-thickness edges.
   Do not subdivide the forehead into many padded locks or add radial spikes.

2. Temple fringe ends abruptly at dark, nearly horizontal ledges immediately
   above the red ties; this is particularly clear in chin_close.png. The reference
   has tapering side-fringe tongues that descend around the ties and continue
   the face framing. The candidate ledges make cap and tied locks look separately
   attached. Their silhouette and shadow boundary support a construction issue;
   pixels cannot distinguish a gap, overlap, or dark receiving surface.
   Continue each outer fringe edge downward into a thin tapered tongue by roughly
   0.05-0.08 Wh and seat the tie/lock root behind that transition. Preserve the
   reference's broad asymmetric center fringe and two forehead openings.

3. The exposed lower face has a longer, nearly level central bottom and firmer
   lower-corner transitions than the soft shallow curve of the canonical face.
   This contributes to a mask/cushion-slab read, but the darker collar contact
   and smooth pale material amplify it. The side image does show the visible
   pale lower-face outline turning backward, so recession is not solely hair
   occlusion. However, the tied lock conceals most of the lower cheek-to-underside
   connection; the pale patch behind it does not prove one continuous recessed
   chin. These images support partial rounding, not a fully demonstrated tucked
   cushion. If revised, favor a broad curved underside and softer corner turn,
   with a small approximately 0.02-0.04 Wh lower-corner lift as a trial; no global
   increase in head depth is justified by this evidence.

The priority is the lower hair-cap construction and temple transition. The
front crown and center bang need continuity, not wholesale hairstyle replacement.
A clear side view of the exposed cushion would resolve the remaining chin
uncertainty; brighter material or more fuzz would not resolve it.
