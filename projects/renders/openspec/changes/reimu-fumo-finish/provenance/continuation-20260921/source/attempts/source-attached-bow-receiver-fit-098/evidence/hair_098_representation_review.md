Keep the094 front face, its graphics, frontal hairline, upper crown, and existing front locks fixed. Replace only the posterior lower construction with **an upper supported cap transitioning into a shallow rear curtain: two broad overlapping laps over a shallow central brown section**. This is a proposed modeling representation, not a claim that the photographs prove three separate manufactured pieces.

Only the concealed upper root strips should conform closely to the head/bow receiving surface. Outside those strips, define broad low-curvature cloth surfaces by their root and free-edge curves; do not copy the lower head Y field or postpone release until the last tip band. Their finite-thickness free edges should remain independently readable through the middle and lower rear. The visible central section must also stop acting as a round closing bowl; otherwise two released side laps still frame a helmet.

Set back the **hidden lower posterior core and corresponding under-cover together only where needed** to fit this intended curtain envelope or remove the visible rounded heel. Keep the core closed, taper the setback into unchanged upper/front support, and maintain brown cloth coverage. Use the desired cloth envelope to bound the setback, not a global head shrink, arbitrary horizontal amputation, or extra outward panel inflation. Segmenting the *visible lower cover's shape and free boundary* is useful; cutting the cushion into disconnected pieces is neither necessary nor supported by the references.

The key trade-off is more consequential than replacing side strips: lower rear support, cover continuity, pile ownership, and side-gusset transitions change together. Too much setback can expose cream, create a hollow-looking head, reveal a hard hinge, or disrupt front framing; too little retains the same closing heel. Free cloth can also increase bow/collar contacts or appear as stiff cards. If a reference-shaped independent curtain is already wholly exterior to the current core and hides it, retain that core instead—setback is an accommodation, not a virtue or a required numerical dose.
