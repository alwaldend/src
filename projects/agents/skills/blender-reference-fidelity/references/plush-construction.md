# Constructed-plush reference guide

Read this guide for stuffed toys, fabric dolls, and Fumo-style models.

## Choose forms by manufacturing logic

- Build the head as a shallow, gusseted cushion with a readable front plane,
  side depth, crown, underside, and rear seam flow. A sphere or egg with hair
  placed over it usually reads as vinyl or anatomy rather than stuffed fabric.
- Keep the torso as a compact stuffed core under constructed clothing. The
  garment silhouette should come from front/back panels, gathers, and stuffing
  pressure, not from a cone hiding a body-shaped volume.
- Make hair locks, bows, sleeves, and garment pieces as thin front/back fabric
  panels with edge thickness, soft fill, seam tension, and deliberate roots.
  Avoid flat cards, cones, radial spikes, and unrelated inflated blobs.
- Treat a hair cap, face opening, and fringe according to their photographed
  construction. A jagged per-polygon material boundary is not a sewn panel,
  while several independently padded forehead pieces often read as armor.
  Validate the complete hair-and-face mass before adding individual locks.
- Fit appliques such as eyes and mouths nearly flush to the evaluated face
  surface. Their projection should be a fabric-layer thickness, not a separate
  pill-shaped volume.
- Seat accessory roots into the receiving seam or cushion. Validate bow roots,
  hair panels, cuffs, hems, and feet from the side and three-quarter views.
- Model feet as simple stuffed pods unless the reference shows anatomy. Tuck
  them behind or beneath the garment by the measured amount; do not invent legs
  to solve placement.
- Distinguish projected overlap from physical intersection. Validate foot and
  hem depth order from the side and three-quarter views and test evaluated
  surfaces for crossings; a correct front silhouette does not excuse clipping.

## Match stuffed behavior

- Use broad, low-frequency compression at contacts and seams. Reserve sharp
  creases for folded fabric edges.
- Preserve slightly flattened planes and asymmetric stuffing. Uniform global
  smoothing produces balloons; random surface noise does not create fabric.
- Let seams change the silhouette or tension of adjacent panels. Decorative
  seam lines without construction consequences do not establish plush form.
- Add fibers, fuzz, stitch detail, and micro-wrinkles only after the neutral
  sculpt passes the multi-view silhouette and construction gates.
- Judge the neutral sculpt as an untextured object, not as abstract topology.
  It must already read as stuffed fabric; if it reads as a helmet, mask, cone,
  robot limb, or disconnected primitives, reject its construction.

## High-impact review order

For character plushes, inspect accessory silhouette, head/face/hair framing,
eye placement, garment and sleeve construction, feet exposure, then secondary
seams and folds. Prioritize the reference features occupying the most screen
area or carrying the character identity.
